01 · Context

Inventory is not a warehouse problem when it blocks a repair

The Inventory management project combines hand-drawn flows with detailed Onsitego service screens containing repair estimates, visit outcomes, service-center assignment and ticket history. That makes inventory part of a broader repair workflow: spare parts, estimates and service outcomes have to stay synchronized with the customer’s job.

The product should treat a spare part as a stateful object in the repair journey—not as a SKU living in a separate inventory module.

FRiction 01

Wrong part, right ticket

A technician can know what the customer needs while the system still lacks a reliable part mapping.

FRiction 02

Stock is visible too late

Availability discovered after a visit causes another appointment, another call and another SLA risk.

FRiction 03

Estimate ≠ inventory

Repair cost, part approval and stock reservation can become three disconnected decisions.

03 · Key insights

Three principles for inventory-service integration

INSIGHT 01

Stateful part tracking

Inventory should track parts as stateful objects moving through the repair journey—requested, reserved, in-transit, consumed—rather than static SKUs in a warehouse system.

INSIGHT 02

Visit outcome as pivot point

The service visit outcome screen is the natural moment to synchronize inventory—consuming reservations when parts are used, releasing them when plans change, and creating follow-up actions for pending replacements.

INSIGHT 03

Decision-surface integration

When technicians select a diagnosis or repair, the system should immediately surface compatible parts, locations, quantities and approval requirements—preventing the "approve now, discover stock later" anti-pattern.

04 · Design principles

Designing for workflow continuity, not module boundaries

The redesign principle centers on following the business event (customer repair) rather than organizational boundaries—so inventory, service tickets and financial systems update together as a cohesive unit rather than requiring manual reconciliation between siloed modules.

05 · Information architecture

From separate modules to unified repair journey

The primary shift was reorganizing the information architecture around the repair job as the central entity, with inventory states, service tickets and financial records as attributes that update together through the job lifecycle rather than existing in separate systems requiring reconciliation.

  • Business-event-driven design (40%)
  • Real-time state synchronization (35%)
  • Technician-centric workflows (25%)

This architectural shift enables:

OUTCOME 01

Reduce repeat visits

Parts availability validated before technician dispatch minimizes unnecessary return trips.

OUTCOME 02

Eliminate manual reconciliation

Automatic state updates eliminate the need for ticket-to-stock matching and correction workflows.

OUTCOME 03

Improve first-time fix rates

Technicians have accurate parts information when making repair decisions and performing service.

06 · Interaction design

State-based inventory modeling for operational clarity

Inventory is modeled around discrete states that map directly to operational actions: REQUESTED (part need identified), RESERVED (stock committed), IN TRANSIT (moving to location), and CONSUMED/RETURNED (job completed with traceability). These states prevent double allocation and enable accurate reconciliation.

07 · Visual language

Color coding and progressive disclosure for repair workflows

A constrained color palette uses semantic meaning: red for critical stockouts or SLA risks, amber for low inventory or warning conditions, green for adequate stock levels, and blue for informational elements. This enables pre-attentive processing—technicians can grasp parts availability at a glance during repair decision-making.

The inventory interface adapts based on repair context, showing relevant parts information without overwhelming the technician with unnecessary warehouse details.
08 · Process

From disconnected modules to one continuous job

The redesign transforms inventory management from a separate warehouse module into an integrated part of the service workflow—so the technician experiences one continuous repair job rather than navigating between disconnected systems for parts, estimates and service outcomes.
