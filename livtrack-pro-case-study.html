Livtrack Pro

Designing the mobile operating layer for service engineers who keep repairs and maintenance moving in the real world.

Role
Product / UX Designer
Users
Service engineers, field agents, ops
Timeline
8–9 months · research to delivery
Scope
Mobile app, job lifecycle, evidence

Because of company policy and NDA constraints, the end-to-end production experience and internal analytics cannot be shown. This case study presents the product problem, research approach, design rationale, and reconstructed interface structure — diagrams below are original illustrations, not reproductions of the shipped screens.

01

The problem

Livtrack Pro is Onsitego's mobile application for internal service engineers and field agents. It supports job tracking, GPS-based movement, media capture, and OTP login. The product sits at the point where a planned service operation becomes real: an engineer must understand the job, reach the customer, diagnose the appliance, capture proof, and update the organization without slowing down the visit.

The design challenge was not simply to add more fields. It was to make a high-pressure, intermittent-connectivity workflow feel obvious, fast, and trustworthy while preserving the operational detail needed by teams who are not physically present.

02

Research across the service ecosystem

I conducted field research and interviews with service-center agents, service engineers, field agents, product managers, business stakeholders, and operations teams. The work ran for approximately 8–9 months from discovery and interviews through synthesis, concept development, interaction design, and delivery.

What I investigated

How jobs are accepted, scheduled and prioritized; where engineers lose time during a visit; which evidence operations needs to trust an update; how exceptions, reassignment and incomplete visits are handled; how location, media and device constraints affect behavior.

Research outputs

Current-state service journey; role and responsibility map; friction inventory across the job lifecycle; opportunity areas for mobile-first simplification; a prioritized workflow and screen model.

03

Key insights

01

The job is a moving target

Engineers need the next best action, not a static record. Status, location, customer context, and exceptions must stay visible as conditions change.

02

Evidence is part of the service

Photos, notes, diagnosis, and visit outcomes are not attachments. They are the proof that allows operations and customers to trust the result.

03

Every extra field has a field cost

Long forms create cognitive load, especially when the engineer is standing, moving, using one hand, or working with a damaged device.

04

The solution direction

I shaped the experience around a clear job lifecycle: prepare → travel → diagnose → act → document → close. Each stage has a focused primary action, while secondary details remain available without competing with the task.

Six-stage service lifecycle used to structure the mobile experience. Every screen in the app maps to exactly one stage, so an engineer never has to guess which part of the job they're looking at.

Designed for the field

Large tap targets, progressive disclosure, short sections, explicit save states, and a clear recovery path for incomplete or interrupted work.

Designed for operations

Structured outcomes, consistent status language, timestamped evidence, and a predictable record that can be consumed by service-center and support teams.

06

Designing for intermittent connectivity

OFFLINE-FIRST CAPTURE

Photos and notes save locally first

Evidence is written to the device immediately and queued for sync, so a dead zone mid-visit never risks losing a diagnosis or photo.

VISIBLE SYNC STATE

The engineer always knows what's saved

A persistent, unobtrusive indicator distinguishes "saved on device" from "synced to operations," so nobody has to guess whether an update went through.

Process

From field research to a six-stage model

01

Shadow the field

Observe engineers on real visits to see where time and attention actually go.

02

Interview the ecosystem

Talk to agents, engineers, PMs and operations so no one team's view sets the model alone.

03

Synthesize frictions

Build a friction inventory across the full job lifecycle, not just the app screens.

04

Define the lifecycle

Collapse the friction inventory into six stages every job must pass through.

05

Design for the hand, not the desk

Prototype with one-handed, standing, gloved-thumb use as the default condition.

06

Deliver with operations in the loop

Validate that every mobile action produces a record operations can trust.

Outcome

A job structure engineers can act on without thinking about the interface, and a data structure operations can trust without chasing engineers for detail.

08

Outcome and reflection

The strongest outcome was a clearer product model: Livtrack Pro is not only a job tracker. It is the operational memory of every service visit. The design work connected the engineer's immediate task with the organization's need for reliable, structured, and explainable service data.

The lifecycle model also became the shared language for the rest of the field-operations suite — the desktop Field Management System and the inventory model both reuse the same stage vocabulary, so a job never has to be re-translated as it moves between a technician's phone and an operator's screen.
