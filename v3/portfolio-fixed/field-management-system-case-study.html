Project context: Enterprise service-operations platform managing hundreds of daily field service jobs across multiple teams and geographies.
01 · Context

Hundreds of jobs, one operations team, no room for ambiguity

The Field Management System + Livtrack 2024 archive contains dense job tables, queue views, reassignment states, modal controls, sorting/filtering patterns and whiteboard system maps. The product reads as an operations command center for coordinating field-service work at scale.

The core UX challenge was not displaying more service data. It was helping an operator answer three questions immediately: What is stuck? Why is it stuck? What can I do about it?

CHALLENGE 01

Information overload

Operators spend excessive time scanning dense tables to find actionable items.

CHALLENGE 02

Context switching

Frequent reassignment creates cognitive overhead and tracking complexity.

CHALLENGE 03

SLA visibility

Critical deadlines are buried in raw data without proactive alerts.

03 · Key insights

Three patterns that define expert operational decision-making

INSIGHT 01

Exception-first scanning

Experts prioritize anomalies over completeness—stuck jobs, SLA risks, and blocking conditions trigger immediate attention before routine processing.

INSIGHT 02

Progressive disclosure

Information is revealed in layers: first the what and why, then the how, and finally the detailed context—matching the operator's natural decision flow.

INSIGHT 03

Action-oriented grouping

Work is organized around intervention types rather than entity types—queues represent actions like "needs assignment" or "awaiting parts" rather than customer or device categories.

04 · Design principles

Designing for operational expertise, not just data display

The redesign principle centers on making the next intervention obvious while preserving the rich context expert operators need for complex decision-making. This means surfacing actionable information first, then enabling progressive disclosure of details as needed.

05 · Information architecture

From entity-based to intervention-based organization

The primary shift was reorganizing the information hierarchy around what operators actually do: identifying stuck work, understanding blocking conditions, and taking corrective action—rather than browsing entities like customers, devices, or jobs in isolation.

  • Exception-first visibility (40%)
  • Progressive detail disclosure (35%)
  • Action-oriented organization (25%)

This architectural shift enables operators to:

OUTCOME 01

Reduce scanning time

Critical items pop out immediately instead of requiring table searches.

OUTCOME 02

Minimize context switches

Related information stays grouped for efficient decision-making.

OUTCOME 03

Improve SLA adherence

Blocking conditions surface before they become critical failures.

06 · Interaction design

Progressive disclosure through contextual detail panels

The detail panel appears contextually when an item is selected, showing relevant information without losing sight of the operational context. This follows the principle of "show me what I need to know, when I need to know it."

07 · Visual language

Color coding and typographic hierarchy for rapid comprehension

A constrained color palette uses semantic meaning: red for critical/SLA-risk items, amber for warnings/blocking conditions, green for normal operations, and blue for informational elements. This enables pre-attentive processing—operators can grasp status at a glance.

The detail panel adapts based on item type and state, showing relevant information without overwhelming the operator with unnecessary fields.
08 · Process

From dense tables to exception-first console

The redesign transforms the operations console from a data-display tool into a decision-support system that surfaces what matters most while preserving access to complete context when needed.

01

02

03

Design

Structure the reassignment modal as a guided decision, and shape saved queues around real operational buckets.

Outcome

An operations console where the next intervention is obvious without hiding the detail an expert operator still needs.
