Livtrack Pro

Designing the mobile operating layer for service engineers who keep repairs and maintenance moving in the real world.

Role
Product / UX Designer
Users
Service engineers, field agents, operations
Timeline
8–9 months · research to design delivery
Scope
Mobile app, job lifecycle, evidence, location
Because of company policy and NDA constraints, the end-to-end production experience and internal analytics cannot be shown. This case study presents the product problem, research approach, design rationale, and an illustrative interface concept in place of real screens.
01

The problem

Livtrack Pro is Onsitego's mobile application for internal service engineers and field agents. It supports job tracking, GPS-based movement, media capture, and OTP login. The product sits at the point where a planned service operation becomes real: an engineer must understand the job, reach the customer, diagnose the appliance, capture proof, and update the organization without slowing down the visit.

The design challenge was not simply to add more fields. It was to make a high-pressure, intermittent-connectivity workflow feel obvious, fast, and trustworthy while preserving the operational detail needed by teams who are not physically present.

02

Research across the service ecosystem

I conducted field research and interviews with service-center agents, service engineers, field agents, product managers, business stakeholders, and operations teams. The work ran for approximately 8–9 months from discovery and interviews through synthesis, concept development, interaction design, and delivery.

What I investigated

  • How jobs are accepted, scheduled, and prioritized
  • Where engineers lose time during a visit
  • Which evidence operations needs to trust an update
  • How exceptions, reassignment, and incomplete visits are handled
  • How location, media, and device constraints affect behavior

Research outputs

  • Current-state service journey
  • Role and responsibility map
  • Friction inventory across the job lifecycle
  • Opportunity areas for mobile-first simplification
  • Prioritized workflow and screen model
03

Key insights

01

The job is a moving target

Engineers need the next best action, not a static record. Status, location, customer context, and exceptions must stay visible as conditions change.

02

Evidence is part of the service

Photos, notes, diagnosis, and visit outcomes are not attachments. They are the proof that allows operations and customers to trust the result.

03

Every extra field has a field cost

Long forms create cognitive load, especially when the engineer is standing, moving, using one hand, or working with a damaged device.

04

The solution direction

I shaped the experience around a clear job lifecycle: prepare → travel → diagnose → act → document → close. Each stage has a focused primary action, while secondary details remain available without competing with the task.

PREPAREReview job, part, customer
TRAVELLive location + ETA
DIAGNOSEGuided fault check
ACTRepair / replace / escalate
DOCUMENTPhotos, notes, signature
CLOSEOutcome + sync

Designed for the field

Large tap targets, progressive disclosure, short sections, explicit save states, and a clear recovery path for incomplete or interrupted work.

Designed for operations

Structured outcomes, consistent status language, timestamped evidence, and a predictable record that can be consumed by service-center and support teams.

Interface concept

Real production screens can't be shown here under NDA, so this is an illustrative reconstruction of the core job-card pattern — the single most-used screen in the app, built around the job lifecycle above rather than a raw data record.

Job card, top to bottom: customer + job status badge, appointment window, service context summary, engineer/customer contact block, primary "Start visit" and secondary "Reschedule" actions, then an evidence tray for the photos required before the visit can be closed.

Illustrative reconstruction for portfolio purposes — not an actual Onsitego production screen.

07

Outcome and reflection

The strongest outcome was a clearer product model: Livtrack Pro is not only a job tracker. It is the operational memory of every service visit. The design work connected the engineer's immediate task with the organization's need for reliable, structured, and explainable service data.

Public reference: Livtrack Pro on Google Play.

Process

From field friction to a working lifecycle

01

Discover

Shadow engineers and service-center agents to see where jobs actually stall.

02

Map

Build the current-state journey and a friction inventory across the job lifecycle.

03

Define

Reframe the product around a six-stage lifecycle instead of a flat record.

04

Design

Prototype job-card, evidence-capture and reassignment flows for one-handed field use.

05

Validate

Review with engineers and operations stakeholders under real field constraints.

06

Deliver

Hand off states, offline behaviour and evidence rules for engineering build.

Outcome

A job lifecycle model that made Livtrack Pro legible as an operational record, not just a task list — and that now underpins how I approach any field-facing workflow.
