Case study 01 · Enterprise · 0→1

AI Assistant for Education

Transforming interactive flat panel displays from underused hardware into a global, subscription-ready classroom AI ecosystem.

My Role
Lead UX Designer — end-to-end
Timeline
Phase 2 & 3 · ~1 year
Team
PMs, AI engineers, UI devs, sales
Reach
6 regions · 108+ languages
01

Overview & business objective

Samsung holds a major global share of the Interactive Flat Panel Display (IFPD) market for classrooms — but the software running on those screens was largely underused. The business objective was to shift the model from selling hardware once to a freemium-to-subscription enterprise SaaS business: a suite of AI-powered teaching tools bundled with the display, sold to schools as an ongoing product.

That suite became the Samsung AI Assistant for Education, and I joined at the start of Phase 2 — after an initial proof-of-concept with no real UX behind it — to take it from raw idea to an end-to-end product deployed in enterprise schools across the USA, Canada, UK, Europe, India, and South Korea.

02

The problem space

Even with a large touchscreen at the front of the room, classroom teaching was still fundamentally analog. Research and VOC feedback surfaced three concrete friction points:

01

The USB bottleneck

Teachers don't get a dedicated personal device per classroom. A lesson meant a personal laptop or flash drive — forget the drive, the lesson stalls.

02

Feature bloat from the POC

Early AI features were buried in nested menus. A teacher can't break eye contact with 40 students to go hunting for a setting.

03

Passive engagement

Students received information but had no real-time, interactive way to be assessed on it during the lesson itself.

How do you design for a "dual-audience" screen — one person operating it up close, forty people reading it from the back of the room — without either one breaking the flow of teaching?

03

The key decision: breaking one menu into two identities

The Phase 1 POC treated every AI feature — Circle to Search, Live Transcript, AI Summary — as items in one flat menu. Based on VOC feedback, I concluded the problem wasn't the features, it was that they had no shared logic for when a teacher would reach for them.

I split the architecture into two distinct identities:

  • On-demand widgets — Circle to Search, Live Caption, and Record Lesson live as floating, always-available actions a teacher can trigger over any content, mid-lesson, without navigating away from what they're teaching.
  • The "My App" hub — a dashboard organized by class and subject, where each lesson holds its recording, AI transcript, and generated artifacts (summaries, quizzes, and future flashcards/video summaries/games) for teachers to review and share.

This mirrors a distinction I'd apply to any interface: actions you take in the moment should never live in the same place as content you manage after the moment — collapsing them into one menu was what made the POC feel bloated in the first place.

04

Solving the hardware problem: AMS & cloud binding

To remove the USB bottleneck, I led the UX for an Account Management System (AMS): a teacher walks up to any Samsung display in the building, signs in with a simple PIN, and their entire teaching environment — profile, preferences, in-progress session — resumes on that screen.

Paired with AMS, I designed a cloud & drive-binding flow so teachers can link Google Drive, local storage, or other connected drives directly inside the app — teaching material lives in the cloud, not on a device that can be forgotten at home.

Why this mattered: the whole "leave your laptop at home" value proposition only holds if identity works at the ecosystem level, not the device level — this decision underpins almost every other feature in the product.
05

Designing for AI trust & active engagement

The assistant runs on Google's Gemini LLM with personalized prompts tuned for classroom use — but introducing AI into K-12 classrooms demands real guardrails, not just capability.

GUARDRAIL 01

Teacher-controlled review gates

Every AI-generated summary or quiz is reviewed by the teacher before it reaches a single student — never auto-broadcast.

GUARDRAIL 02

Live AI quizzes

AI generates a contextual quiz from the live transcript; the teacher shows a QR code on the IFPD, students answer in real time on their own device.

GUARDRAIL 03

Distance-readable UI

Text, contrast, and component sizing tuned so a student in the back row can read prompts and alerts at a glance.

06

The localization challenge

Deploying across six regions meant supporting 108+ languages — and localization here wasn't a translation task, it was a core UX constraint. The system language a teacher navigates in often needed to differ from the language they were actively teaching in (a Spanish-speaking teacher running an English lesson, for example).

Rather than exposing that complexity as another settings menu, I separated system language from input/teaching language as two independent controls. I also authored a full UX glossary — every interface string and error message, rewritten for clarity — handed to translation teams so that, for instance, a string translated into German wouldn't break a layout meant to be read from twenty feet away.

07

Ecosystem breadth & future roadmap

The product isn't one app — it's an ecosystem, each surface built for a different person in the school:

📺

IFPD app

The in-classroom experience — widgets, live captioning, quizzes.

🧑‍🏫

Teacher app

Manage content and study material outside class, on any device.

🛠️

Admin app

Device, user, NFC, subscription and credit management at the school level.

🎓

Student app (POC)

Future roadmap — the closed personalized-learning loop below.

The student experience closes the loop: students access shared lessons and summaries, take the live quiz, and — based on where they got questions wrong — the system is designed to generate personalized study material, flashcards, and AI podcasts targeting exactly what they're missing.

08

Impact

6
regions deployed — USA, Canada, UK, Europe, India, South Korea
108+
languages supported
3
connected apps — teacher, admin, IFPD (+ student POC)
09

Takeaway

The most advanced AI model is useless if the interface adds friction for the person using it. Reducing hardware dependence, surfacing tools as in-the-moment widgets instead of buried menu items, and treating localization as a UX problem rather than a translation task — that's what turned a rough proof-of-concept into an enterprise-ready product shipping globally.

Case study 02 · Enterprise · Agentic UX

Next-Gen Agentic Customer Support Platform

Replacing fragmented legacy workflows with four interconnected, explainable-AI portals for 25,000+ support staff across India and global markets.

My Role
Lead UX Designer
Scale
25,000+ staff
Timeline
6 months, phase-wise
Key outcome
60–80% time saved
01

The problem: four sources of operational friction

Agents, field engineers, and legal staff were absorbing the cognitive load a system should have carried. Research and shadowing surfaced four concrete bottlenecks:

01

High cognitive load

Agents cross-referenced 4–5 disconnected portals per single ticket, hunting for purchase history and telemetry data by hand.

02

Slow hardware triage

Line-On-Display (LOD) screen cases took 10–15 minutes per device to photograph via third-party camera apps and compile into manual PDF reports.

03

Image quality rejections

15–20% of daily hardware cases were rejected instantly for motion blur or glare — restarting a multi-day repair cycle each time.

04

Paper-heavy legal CCM

Manual data entry from physical court notices, paper courier tracking for Vakalatnama forms, and disconnected communication with external lawyers.

02

The paradigm shift: human-in-the-loop, not autonomous

The core UX decision underneath all four portals: AI never acts alone. It synthesizes context and calculates a confidence score, presents it through a generative UI with evidence attached, and a human verifies and executes with one click.

    03

    Four interconnected portals

    One master AI orchestrator, four role-specific interfaces — each tailored to how that person actually works.

    Field Engineers & SAW Inspection Agents

    📱 Nirnay — AI-Guided Hardware Triage

    Real-time on-device camera guidance with computer vision feedback, blocking blurred uploads before submission and routing verified defects automatically.

    • On-device real-time camera gate — spatial framing guidance to eliminate glare
    • Pre-submission AI check blocks blurry images at point of capture
    • Confidence-based auto-routing: high confidence auto-approves, low confidence triggers 1-click human review
    VOC Support Representatives

    💬 Priority Desk — One-Click VOC Resolution

    Consolidates device history, chat logs, and sentiment analysis into a single timeline — replacing what used to require gathering details across 5 separate tabs.

    • Predictive recommendations with confidence percentage and supporting evidence
    • Auto-generated communication drafts (SMS/email) ready for one-click send
    Measured pilot impact: 60–80% reduction in VOC comprehension time, +112% SLA case closure rate.
    Legal Employee Reps & External Counsel

    ⚖️ Sahay — Legal Case Management

    Modernized paper-heavy legal dispute operations via government e-Jagruti API integration and automated OCR notice ingestion.

    • OCR scans physical legal notices into digital records in seconds
    • AI legal risk sensing recommends Contest vs. Settle/Dismiss from historical rulings
    • 1-click Vakalatnama authorization & courier dispatch routing
    • Smart Next-Date-of-Hearing briefing calendar for external counsel
    SSO Command Center for 25,000+ Staff

    🛡️ CS Agentic Hub — Master Orchestrator

    The central sign-on gateway and orchestrator. Staff construct custom UI workspaces on the fly using natural-language prompts, without leaving their command center.

    • Daily Smart AI Planner splits workload into autonomous background tasks vs. items needing human approval
    • Generative UI renders live widgets from prompts like "show pending LOD approvals" or "render my CSAT summary"
    04

    Impact

    −70%
    VOC creation time (~15 min → 3 min)
    +112%
    SLA closure rate
    ~0%
    hardware triage rejection (from 15–20%)
    25k+
    active enterprise staff

    Average handling time — legacy vs. agentic

    Minutes spent per ticket across portals, before vs. after.

    Hardware rejection drop vs. SLA growth

    Six-month phase-wise rollout, pilot to scale.
    05

    Research insights & explainable AI trust

    INSIGHT 01

    Fatigued gatherers, not slow decision-makers

    Shadowing showed agents spent 70% of handling time just searching portals for context — so the fix was evidence-backed summary cards, not a better search tool.

    INSIGHT 02

    AI trust is fragile

    Agents ignored suggestions they couldn't see the reasoning behind. Every recommendation now shows an explicit confidence score, e.g. "94% confidence, based on 1,200 past tickets."

    INSIGHT 03

    One rigid UI fails every role

    Engineers need fast photo flows; lawyers need court briefings. Generative UI lets each role reshape their own workspace instead of forcing one layout on everyone.

    That second insight became a routing rule applied across every portal:

    AUTO / 1-CLICK
    High confidence (85–100%)

    Recommendation is pre-drafted from complete telemetry; agent executes with a single verification click.

    IN-REVIEW
    Low confidence (<85%)

    System flags missing data or discrepancies and routes directly to a human agent for manual verification.

    ON-DEMAND UI
    Generative workspace

    Natural-language prompts render custom widgets on demand, so agents query history without leaving their hub.

    06

    Takeaway

    Every one of these portals solves the same underlying problem in a different costume: people were doing a machine's job of gathering evidence, and a machine was being trusted to do a human's job of deciding. Splitting those two apart — AI gathers and proposes, a human decides and executes — is what made 25,000 people trust a system they'd never fully hand control to.

    Independent project · UX Research · 6 weeks

    Skinthesis

    A research-led exploration into safer, more personalized skincare in India — connecting market evidence, user behaviour, professional guidance and product opportunity.

    My Role
    UX Researcher / Product Designer
    Scope
    Research → synthesis → concept → UX
    Platform
    Mobile-first concept
    Duration
    6 weeks
    01

    Overview

    Skinthesis started as an exploration of India's fast-growing skincare and wellness landscape. The research progressively narrowed the opportunity toward a more specific problem: people want healthier skin, but often lack confidence in product selection, diagnosis, routine-building, and follow-up care.

    6
    weeks of project work
    73
    responses in the QIA survey
    15
    structured user interviews
    02

    A layered research strategy

    Structured to reduce ambiguity at each stage: understand the market, classify the opportunity, validate user attributes, investigate behaviour, then synthesize evidence into actionable problems.

    111
    mapped qualitative influential attributes
    23
    exploratory survey responses
    15
    structured interviews

    The shift was from "which skincare features should we build?" to "which user problems are strong enough to justify a product?"

    Market and survey statistics in this research are drawn from the original study; figures attributed to third-party market reports should be independently re-verified before being published as fact.

    03

    What the research converged on

    • Lack of personal knowledge and confidence — knowing skincare matters isn't the same as knowing how to interpret ingredients or build a routine
    • Lack of personalized attention — a gap between generic recommendations and advice that accounts for individual skin, lifestyle and concerns
    • Misleading product suitability claims — labels like "for dry skin" don't reliably translate into a confident decision
    • Trial-and-error behaviour — low confidence leads to inconsistent routines shaped by social content and word of mouth
    • Difficulty sustaining care — even a chosen routine needs reminders, follow-up and a way to adapt over time

    The opportunity isn't simply "personalized recommendations." It's a system that helps people make, understand, follow and revisit safer skincare decisions.

    04

    Persona

    Representative user story

    "I want to understand my skin before I spend money on products."

    Young, urban, research-oriented skincare consumer

    Aware of skincare and researches before purchasing, but still relies on trial-and-error when confidence runs out. Values sunscreen and professional advice, and wants personalized recommendations rather than sales-led guidance.

    05

    From problem to product system

    Understand

    Skin profile, concerns, lifestyle, stress, sleep, preferences and constraints.

    Decide

    Ingredient analysis, suitability verification, product recommendations and professional consultation.

    Act

    Personalized routines, reminders, hydration tracking, product usage tracking.

    Learn

    Natural skincare education, preventive-care content, community experiences, myth reduction.

    Connect

    Certified dermatologist access, appointment scheduling and regular follow-ups.

    Adapt

    Feedback loops that let the routine evolve with the user's skin and lifestyle.

    06

    Feature concepts

    • Tiered subscription plans — professional and personalized care at multiple budget levels, instead of one service model for everyone
    • Personalized skincare routine — generated around skin type, concerns and lifestyle, with customizable steps and frequency
    • Product suitability verification — scan a barcode or QR code to surface ingredients, intended skin type, and potential concerns
    • Virtual dermatologist consultation — connects users to professionals when the problem needs expertise, not another product
    • Progress, product & hydration tracking — lightweight reminders and tracking to sustain routines over time
    07

    Reflection

    The central experience principle became confidence — confidence in understanding one's skin, choosing products, following a routine, and knowing when to seek professional help. Next steps would include source verification for every market claim, stronger clinical guardrails, dermatologist validation, accessibility testing, and a sharper MVP focused on suitability + routine + professional escalation.

    Freelance · Discovery → UI

    WebFixxers UX Overhaul

    A research-led redesign focused on clearer services, transparent pricing, stronger trust signals, and a frictionless path to contact.

    My Role
    UX / UI Designer
    Scope
    Discovery → UI
    Platform
    Responsive web
    Client
    Freelance
    01

    The challenge

    Small and medium-sized businesses often struggle to find a comprehensive yet user-friendly way to establish and grow their online presence. Visitors needed clear service offerings, transparent pricing, credible proof, and an easy way to start a conversation.

    02

    Research: quantitative + qualitative

    Interviews with prospective small-business owners, startup founders and marketing leads surfaced recurring concerns around pricing, trust, time, and post-launch support.

    70%
    required both web design and SEO services
    60%
    left when pricing wasn't easy to find
    50%
    of leads came through referral-style trust signals
    85%
    preferred clear, minimal navigation

    The strongest insight was not "add more." It was "make it easier to understand."

    03

    Persona

    User persona

    Sarah Matthews

    Owner of a local bakery
    About

    Tech-aware, time-poor, and focused on growing a local business. She knows digital presence matters but doesn't want to become an SEO or web-dev expert.

    Pain points
    • Technical overwhelm
    • Limited time
    • Budget uncertainty
    • Fear of hidden costs
    04

    Five whys: why were visitors dropping off?

    Visitors were dropping off at pricing without making contact.
    Because the pricing options were confusing.
    Because service tiers weren't clearly differentiated.
    Because generic descriptions lumped too many services together.
    Because "comprehensive" was prioritized over easy comparison.
    Root cause → clearer labels and straightforward tier distinctions.
    05

    What shipped

    • Clear service architecture — content reorganized around user intent instead of an internal service list
    • Transparent package comparison — tiers distinguished clearly enough to compare without contacting sales first
    • Trust signals closer to conversion — testimonials and proof placed where hesitation was highest
    • Responsive, mobile-first behavior across the full site
    06

    Reflection

    Good UX is often subtraction. When a service is inherently complex, the job isn't to expose every detail at once — it's to reveal the right information at the right moment, build confidence, and make the next action obvious.
