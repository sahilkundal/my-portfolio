01 / Samsung · Enterprise AI · 0→1

AI Assistant for Education

Transforming interactive flat panel displays from underused hardware into a global, subscription-ready classroom AI ecosystem.

Role
Lead UX Designer, end to end
Scope
Teacher, admin, and classroom experiences
Reach
6 regions · 108+ languages
Timeline
Phase 2 & 3 · ~1 year

From hardware to a connected classroom service

Interactive classroom displays were capable, but much of their software remained underused. Teachers still depended on a personal laptop or flash drive, while early AI tools sat inside nested menus and gave students few ways to participate in real time.

The opportunity was to turn a one-time hardware sale into an ongoing classroom product without interrupting the flow of teaching or making the AI feel like a black box.

Separate what happens now from what happens next

  • Made in-the-moment tools available as floating widgets over any lesson content.
  • Created a class-and-subject hub for recordings, transcripts, summaries, and quizzes teachers can review and share.
  • Designed account and cloud-binding flows so a teacher could resume their environment on any classroom display.
  • Kept AI-generated work under teacher review, and treated system language and teaching language as separate controls.
Product reach

The ecosystem was deployed across the USA, Canada, UK, Europe, India, and South Korea, supporting 108+ languages across connected IFPD, teacher, and admin experiences.

02 / Samsung · Enterprise · Agentic UX

Next-Gen Agentic Customer Support Platform

Replacing fragmented legacy workflows with four interconnected, explainable-AI portals for 25,000+ support staff across India and global markets.

Role
Lead UX Designer
Scope
4 role-specific portals
Scale
25,000+ support staff
Delivery
Phase-wise · 6 months

Reduce the work around the work

Agents, field engineers, and legal teams were carrying the cognitive load of disconnected systems. A single case could require cross-checking several portals, manually compiling evidence, or re-entering information from paper notices.

The interaction model treats AI as a synthesis and guidance layer, not an autonomous decision-maker: show the recommendation, expose its evidence and confidence, then let a person verify and act.

One orchestrator, four focused experiences

  • Nirnay: on-device image guidance and quality checks for hardware triage.
  • Priority Desk: a consolidated customer timeline with evidence-backed recommendations and message drafts.
  • Sahay: digitized legal case workflows, notice ingestion, and counsel coordination.
  • CS Agentic Hub: a central sign-on and workspace for tasks that need human approval.
Reported pilot signals

The source study reports a 60–80% reduction in VOC comprehension time and a 112% increase in SLA case closure rate during the pilot. These are pilot measures, not a guarantee of results across every workflow.

03 / Onsitego · Design systems · 2021—2025

ELAN — building a design system for a service ecosystem

A centralized Figma design system that turned consumer, field-service, retail, and enterprise products into a reusable visual and interaction language.

Role
UX/UI Designer + system owner
Scope
3 core apps · 5–6 portals and dashboards
Tool
Figma
Timeline
Dec 2021 — Jan 2025

Make familiar interactions predictable

Products across the service ecosystem repeatedly solved the same interface problems in different ways. Buttons, forms, status patterns, navigation, tables, and imagery drifted between teams—adding design debt and making cross-product work harder to learn.

Rather than begin with an abstract component library, I audited existing products, grouped duplicates, and separated shared behaviour from product-specific needs.

Build from foundations to product patterns

  • Established shared color, type, spacing, icon, imagery, and hierarchy foundations.
  • Consolidated common controls and their focus, disabled, error, and success states.
  • Connected components to real service-request, dashboard, form, and field-work patterns.
  • Kept the library in the everyday Figma workflow so shipped product needs could feed improvements back into the system.
System in use

The system supported 3 core applications and 5–6 enterprise portals or dashboards. Broader product outcomes reported in the original study relate to work using the system and should not be attributed to ELAN alone.

04 / Independent · Design automation

Designing my own workflow

An AI-assisted annotation engine that closes the gap between a coded interface and its original UX specification.

Role
Design automation · solo build
Timeline
2025—
Tools
Cline · React / HTML / CSS
Focus
Design-to-development handoff

Turn review from transcription into judgment

Comparing a built interface with its UX specification meant manually re-reading requirements, checking screens, and documenting gaps. The repeated work was slow, easy to vary between reviewers, and vulnerable to spec drift.

Fixes then had to be rewritten as separate tickets, often losing context between the review and the work queue.

Connect the steps that were previously separate

  • Compare a coded interface with the intent in its UX specification.
  • Generate structured annotations for review instead of asking reviewers to transcribe every difference.
  • Generate workflow and architecture diagrams from code.
  • Connect review output to Jira card creation, assignment, and status recommendations.
Reflection

The tool is intended to move designer time from repetitive documentation toward decisions about what matters. It applies the same systems principle as a component library: identify repeated work, find the reusable rule, then automate the routine part.

05 / Independent · UX research · 6 weeks

Skinthesis

A research-led exploration into safer, more personalized skincare in India—connecting market evidence, user behaviour, professional guidance, and product opportunity.

Role
UX Researcher / Product Designer
Research
73 survey responses · 15 interviews
Scope
Research → synthesis → product concept
Platform
Mobile-first concept

Confidence was the central user need

People wanted healthier skin, but often lacked confidence in product selection, diagnosis, routine-building, and follow-up. Market exploration and user research progressively narrowed the question from which features to build to which problems justified a product.

The study mapped 111 influential user attributes and combined exploratory research, a larger survey, and structured interviews to understand needs and behaviour.

Connect understanding, decisions, and ongoing care

  • Help people understand skin concerns, lifestyle, and constraints.
  • Support suitability checks and product decisions with clearer information.
  • Make personalized routines easier to follow with lightweight reminders and progress tracking.
  • Provide a path to certified dermatologists when a concern needs professional advice.
Product direction

A connected concept for understanding, deciding, acting, learning, connecting with professionals, and adapting over time—with clinical validation and stronger source verification identified as next steps.

06 / Freelance · Discovery to UI

WebFixxers UX Overhaul

A research-led redesign focused on clearer services, transparent pricing, stronger trust signals, and a frictionless path to contact.

Role
UX / UI Designer
Scope
Discovery → responsive web UI
Audience
Small-business owners and founders
Focus
Service clarity and enquiry journey

Make an unfamiliar service easier to evaluate

Prospective customers needed to understand what was offered, what it might cost, and whether the provider could support them after launch. Research surfaced recurring concerns about pricing, trust, time, and technical overwhelm.

The root issue was not a shortage of information; service tiers and generic descriptions made the important differences difficult to compare.

Reduce uncertainty before the contact step

  • Organized the service architecture around visitor intent instead of an internal service list.
  • Made package differences and pricing easier to compare.
  • Placed testimonials and proof closer to moments of hesitation.
  • Designed responsive, mobile-first behaviour across the site.
Research signals

The original study reports that 60% of respondents left when pricing was hard to find and 85% preferred clear, minimal navigation. These figures describe that project research, not a universal benchmark.
